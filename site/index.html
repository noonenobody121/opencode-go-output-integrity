START HERE / TWO-MINUTE BRIEFING

The hypothesis—and what the evidence supports.

Quantization reduces the memory and compute needed to serve AI models. Very aggressive reductions can harm generation quality. The question is whether this contributed to problems observed on a paid OpenCode Go route.

H₁
Research hypothesisReduced-precision inference may have contributed to failures in this paid OpenCode Go case.
Why investigate?

Repeated output degeneration, unexpected reasoning delimiters and reports of better results from other providers resemble some failure modes studied in low-bit inference research.

What would confirm it?

Verified deployment precision, model revision, and controlled same-prompt tests that isolate weight precision from client, context, tokenizer and decoding differences.

What is not proven: That OpenCode Go used quantized weights at all—or any specific 2-bit, 3-bit or 4-bit configuration. Symptom similarity does not identify the cause.

How can an answer go missing?

A model may return separate fields for reasoning and the final answer. If answer text lands in the reasoning field, a client may have no ordinary final answer to show.

05 / CAUSE AND ALTERNATIVES

Could quantization explain it?

The hypothesis deserves testing—especially given the repetition and long-context symptoms. But a similar-looking failure mode in a scientific paper is not identification of OpenCode's production model.

H₁

Aggressive quantization / model-serving instability

Candidate explanation · Unverified for this deployment
PLAUSIBLE ≠ PROVEN

Compressing model weights can change token probabilities. At sufficiently aggressive settings, published studies report degradation in long-context accuracy, generation quality and—in some extreme 2-bit reasoning systems—runaway repetitive traces. That creates a scientifically grounded failure-mode analogy for the observed OpenCode Go output.

01
Matching symptom

Long repetitive text and poorly terminated reasoning occur in the case export and in some low-bit research.

02
Missing link

No published OpenCode Go deployment precision, weight format, exact backend revision, or controlled full-precision comparison has been verified.

03
Testable prediction

If precision contributes, identical requests with matched context and decoding settings should show materially worse looping or channel reliability on the suspected deployment than on a verified baseline.

COMPETING EXPLANATIONSclick to inspect
DOCUMENTED IN LOCAL ADAPTER

Tagged history replay

One MCC configuration replayed earlier reasoning as literal tags, and requested max effort was mapped to high. Either could change subsequent model behavior without proving weight quantization.

!

Critical distinction: The public claim is not “OpenCode Go secretly uses 2-bit models.” The claim is that observed failures may be consistent with quantization-related instability, among other causes, and warrant transparent investigation and controlled benchmarking.

06 / THE SCIENTIFIC RECORD

What published research says.

Five linked sources: peer-reviewed studies and a clearly labeled preprint. They support the mechanisms; none examines this particular OpenCode Go deployment.

01
EMNLP 2025 PEER REVIEWED

Does quantization affect models’ performance on long-context tasks?

Mekala et al. · 9,700 examples, 5 methods, 5 models. Some 4-bit configurations lost up to 59% accuracy; effects varied greatly by model, method and task.

Relevance: long contexts and model/method dependence.
↗ 02
ARXIV · JUN 2026 PREPRINT

Extreme Low-Bit Inference in Reasoning Models: Failure Modes and Targeted Recovery

Alimaskina et al. · In Qwen3 reasoning models, extreme 2-bit inference produced extended repetitive traces, delayed commitment and unclosed reasoning segments.

Relevance: the most direct looping analogy; different model and precision unverified here.
↗ 03
ICML 2026 PEER REVIEWED

LFQ: Logit-aware Final-block Quantization for Boosting the Generation Quality of Low-Bit Quantized LLMs

Lee et al. · Low-bit quantization can misalign token probabilities and impair longer generations and extended reasoning, even when basic language-model tests appear healthy.

Relevance: why short benchmarks might miss long-form reliability losses.
↗ 04
IJCAI 2025 PEER REVIEWED

Exploring the Trade-Offs: Quantization Methods, Task Difficulty, and Model Size

Lee et al. · Quantization effects vary substantially; their coding, STEM and instruction-following evaluations showed significant sensitivity in some settings.

Relevance: agentic coding is a task-specific test, not just perplexity.
↗ 05
ACL 2025 PEER REVIEWED

Learning to Generate Structured Output with Schema Reinforcement Learning

Lu et al. · Large language models can fail at complex valid-JSON generation even without any identified quantization issue.

Relevance: an important alternative to quantization-only claims.
↗
✳

Interpretation rule: A finding such as “up to 59% loss” belongs to specific experiments and must not be presented as a measured quality loss for OpenCode Go. We report it as scientific context only.

07 / REPRODUCIBILITY PROTOCOL

How to test the theory fairly.

These are proposed controls, not tests the public site claims were already completed. The objective is to isolate model-serving differences from adapter, prompt and client effects.

01

Match the model and revision. Verify the underlying weights/version and not just a shared marketing name.

02

Fix the input and environment. Same prompt, system instructions, tools, schema, context length and replay structure.

03

Fix the decoding controls. Match effort, output budget, stop conditions, streaming and supported sampling settings.

04

Capture raw fields separately. Preserve reasoning_content, content, tool calls, stop reasons and native stream events—without exposing credentials.

05

Repeat across provider × harness. Run multiple randomized replicates, include simple and long-context tasks, and independently grade outcomes.

06

Publish confidence intervals. Report counts, denominators, missing cases, and error rates. Separate formatting validity from semantic correctness.

09 / DISCLOSURES & RESPONSIBLE SHARING

What this case cannot prove.

What this site is

An independent, single-case incident analysis assembled from user-supplied screenshots, a JSON session export and a locally authored investigation package. It is not an official vendor report, a statistical sample of customers or a confirmed root-cause analysis.

What has not been demonstrated

No direct evidence of OpenCode's production quantization bit width, weight precision, provider infrastructure or exact upstream model revision. No controlled, matching raw traces from competing providers. No vendor certification of the local SQLite investigation.

Why the raw files are not public

Full session exports contain unrelated project content and potentially identifying paths, session details and workflow material. We publish derived, redacted findings and a cropped status strip, not unreviewed logs or private legal documents.

Corrections and responses

A provider response, a replication, or a correction should be incorporated with equal prominence. The most useful outcome is identifying the failure layer. Suggest a correction on GitHub using redacted evidence only.

HELP REPRODUCE RESPONSIBLY

Bring traces. Not assumptions.

Share sanitized raw-field comparisons, exact revision information, or controlled failure rates. Do not publish API keys, unrelated user data, or hidden prompts.
